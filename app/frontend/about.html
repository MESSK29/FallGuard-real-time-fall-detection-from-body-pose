Project Overview & Ethics

The FallGuard Mission & Technical Design

Designing intelligent elder care assistance that prioritizes human dignity, robust detection, and uncompromising data privacy.

The Problem: Falls and the "Long Lie"

Falls represent the leading cause of accidental injury and trauma among adults aged 65 and older worldwide. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), over 684,000 fatal falls occur annually, with adults over 60 suffering the greatest proportion of fatal injuries.

However, in elder care environments, an equally grave consequence is what geriatric specialists term the "Long Lie"—remaining on the floor for more than an hour after a fall due to being injured, disoriented, or unable to reach a phone or pull-cord.

  • Severe Medical Complications: Prolonged immobility on a cold floor frequently causes hypothermia, dehydration, pressure ulcers, and acute rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown leading to kidney damage).
  • Dramatically Increased Mortality: Research indicates that individuals experiencing a long lie of over an hour have a 6-month mortality rate approaching 50%, even when no initial bone fracture occurred.
  • Wearable Pendant Failure: Up to 80% of seniors do not consistently wear alarm pendants (especially during nighttime bathroom visits, where fall risks peak), or forget to press the alert button when panic or trauma sets in.

Our Approach: Pure Edge Kinematics

FallGuard replaces cumbersome wearables and invasive video surveillance with a lightweight, vision-based edge pipeline. Instead of processing heavy pixels through power-hungry neural networks on cloud servers, FallGuard runs entirely on the host laptop CPU:

  • MediaPipe Pose Landmark Extraction: Tracks 33 anatomical landmarks per frame on CPU at ~30 FPS.
  • Torso Length Normalization: To make calculations invariant to whether the person is standing close to or far from the camera, all distances and velocities are normalized by the individual's torso length (distance between shoulder midpoint and hip midpoint).
  • Engineered Kinematic Features: We compute torso tilt relative to gravity, vertical hip drop velocity, and bounding aspect ratio in real time.
  • 2-Second Sliding Window Statistics: Captures the dynamic transition from standing/walking into rapid descent and subsequent post-fall stillness.
  • Dual-Stage Decision Logic: A machine learning model (XGBoost or Random Forest) detects the fall event, which triggers a secondary 2-second verification countdown to confirm the individual is incapacitated and lying still.

Privacy Architecture: Zero Raw Video Transmission

Elderly individuals deserve privacy and autonomy in their own living spaces. Installing standard security cameras in bedrooms, bathrooms, and private corridors is often rejected as intrusive surveillance.

FallGuard is built with privacy by architecture, not by policy. No raw image or video clip is ever stored on disk, sent across the network, or uploaded to any server.

Factor Traditional CCTV / Cloud Cam FallGuard System
Raw Video Storage Stored on NVR disk / Cloud storage Never stored (0 bytes saved)

Limitations & Edge Cases

As an academic project, FallGuard provides high interpretability and efficiency, but users and evaluators must understand its operational constraints:

  • Severe Visual Occlusion: If a person falls behind high-backed furniture, a kitchen counter, or becomes completely concealed beneath heavy bedding, optical landmarks cannot be captured.
  • Intentional Low-Lying Postures: Floor exercises (yoga, stretching) or playing with pets on the floor may resemble a fall state if the person remains static, though normal breathing/arm adjustments mitigate this.
  • Lighting & Contrast Conditions: MediaPipe relies on standard optical cameras. In pitch-black rooms, pose detection degrades without infrared or ambient night-lighting.
  • Acted vs. Real-World Fall Dynamics: The model is trained and benchmarked on volunteer-enacted fall datasets (e.g., UR Fall, Le2i, or synthetic runs). Real elderly falls often exhibit distinct physiological dynamics (e.g., stumbling, grasping for handrails, slow sliding falls) that require ongoing dataset diversity.

Non-Medical Prototype

FallGuard is an educational student research project. It is a prototype tested on acted falls and is NOT a certified medical device, diagnostic tool, or replacement for professional caregiver supervision.
